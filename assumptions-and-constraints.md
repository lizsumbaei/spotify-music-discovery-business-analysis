# Assumptions and Constraints — Spotify Music Discovery & Personalised Recommendation System

> **Case Study Context:** This document forms part of an academic Business Analysis case study. The assumptions and constraints below support a hypothetical/proposed solution inspired by Spotify's music discovery context. They do not represent Spotify's actual internal assumptions, constraints or business requirements.

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the assumptions and constraints used to define the scope of the proposed music discovery and personalised recommendation solution.

Documenting assumptions and constraints helps establish the boundaries of the analysis and makes clear where further investigation would be required.

---

## 2. Assumptions

An assumption is something treated as true for the purpose of the case study but which would require validation in a real-world project.

### AS-001 — User Interaction Information

It is assumed that relevant listener interaction information would be available to support the proposed recommendation process.

### AS-002 — User Participation

It is assumed that listeners would interact with the proposed music discovery experience sufficiently to provide useful recommendation signals.

### AS-003 — Recommendation Purpose

It is assumed that the primary purpose of the proposed solution is to improve music discovery and provide more relevant recommendations.

### AS-004 — Creator Visibility

It is assumed that improving discovery opportunities for relevant music creators represents a potential business objective.

### AS-005 — Data Governance

It is assumed that any information used by the proposed solution would be subject to appropriate privacy, security and data-governance requirements.

### AS-006 — Stakeholder Availability

It is assumed that appropriate stakeholders would be available to validate business needs, requirements and proposed solution outcomes in a real-world implementation.

### AS-007 — Requirement Validation

It is assumed that proposed requirements can be reviewed and validated before being approved for implementation.

### AS-008 — Supported Platforms

It is assumed that the proposed experience would operate across defined supported platforms, although the specific platforms have not been established within this case study.

---

## 3. Constraints

A constraint is a limitation that may restrict the proposed solution, project or analysis.

### CON-001 — Case Study Scope

This project is an academic Business Analysis case study and does not represent a real Spotify project.

### CON-002 — Limited Organisational Information

The analysis does not have access to Spotify's internal systems, processes, policies, business data or confidential information.

### CON-003 — No Confirmed Stakeholder Interviews

The requirements and stakeholder analysis are based on the defined case-study scenario rather than confirmed interviews with Spotify stakeholders.

### CON-004 — Technical Scope

The project focuses on Business Analysis rather than software development or technical implementation.

### CON-005 — Recommendation Algorithm

The specific recommendation algorithm and technical implementation have not been defined.

### CON-006 — Data Availability

The case study does not contain a confirmed production dataset or verified user interaction dataset.

### CON-007 — Legal and Regulatory Validation

Specific legal, privacy and regulatory requirements have not been fully assessed and would require specialist validation in a real-world project.

### CON-008 — Undefined Performance Thresholds

Specific performance, availability and scalability targets have not been established because the necessary technical and business information is outside the scope of this case study.

### CON-009 — Scope Changes

Changes to the business problem, stakeholder needs or proposed solution could require corresponding changes to the documented requirements and traceability matrix.

---

## 4. Assumptions vs Constraints

The distinction between assumptions and constraints is important in Business Analysis.

| Assumptions                                            | Constraints                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Treated as true for the purpose of analysis            | Limit what can be done                        |
| Require validation                                     | Must be considered when defining the solution |
| May change as more information becomes available       | May require mitigation or management          |
| Example: relevant interaction information is available | Example: no access to Spotify's internal data |

---

## 5. Risks Created by Assumptions

Assumptions may create risks if they are later shown to be incorrect.

For example:

| Assumption                                    | Potential Risk                                                             | Possible Response                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Relevant interaction information is available | Recommendations may not have sufficient information to operate effectively | Validate data availability and requirements  |
| Users provide sufficient interaction signals  | Recommendation relevance may be limited                                    | Investigate alternative interaction signals  |
| Stakeholders are available                    | Requirements may not be adequately validated                               | Identify stakeholder availability early      |
| Data can be used appropriately                | Privacy or governance issues may arise                                     | Conduct privacy and data-governance analysis |
| Supported platforms can be identified         | Compatibility requirements may remain unclear                              | Confirm platform scope                       |

---

## 6. Impact on Requirements

Assumptions and constraints should be reviewed when requirements are developed or changed.

For example:

* A data-availability constraint may affect functional requirements relating to recommendations.
* Privacy constraints may affect requirements relating to interaction data.
* Technical scope constraints may prevent detailed implementation requirements from being defined.
* Lack of confirmed stakeholder input may mean that requirements remain proposed rather than approved.

---

## 7. Business Analysis Considerations

In a real Business Analysis project, assumptions and constraints would be reviewed throughout the project lifecycle.

The Business Analyst would:

1. Identify assumptions and constraints.
2. Confirm their source.
3. Validate assumptions with appropriate stakeholders.
4. Assess their potential impact.
5. Record changes.
6. Update affected requirements.
7. Maintain requirements traceability.

This approach helps prevent undocumented assumptions from becoming hidden requirements or project risks.

The project demonstrates analysis techniques and documentation practices without claiming access to Spotify's internal information, systems, processes or stakeholders.
